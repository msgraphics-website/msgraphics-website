The glass front is the biggest blank surface most showrooms have. It is also the one people walk past every day on a busy road in Rajouri Garden, Lajpat Nagar or Sector 18 in Noida. Done well, window graphics tell people what you sell and make the store look finished. Done badly, they make a showroom look closed, dark or cluttered.

There are three main materials to choose from, and a few checks that make a big difference.

## Frosted film

Frosted film gives clear glass a sandblasted, etched look. It lets light through but blurs what is behind it.

**Use it for:** privacy at eye level, offices and cabins inside a showroom, clinic and salon fronts, trial rooms, and safety bands on large glass doors so people do not walk into them.

**Ways to use it:**

- **Plain frosted band** across the glass at a set height
- **Frosted film with your logo or pattern cut out**, so the clear parts show the design
- **Cut frosted letters or patterns** on otherwise clear glass
- **Printed frosted film**, where colour is added to part of the film

Frosted film looks calm and premium. It is a good choice when you want branding without hiding the showroom inside.

## One-way vision

One-way vision is a perforated vinyl. From outside, people see a full printed graphic. From inside, staff and customers can still look out through the tiny holes.

**Use it for:** large glass fronts where you want a bold, full-colour graphic, such as a seasonal campaign or a product image, without blocking the view out.

**What to keep in mind:**

- It works best when the outside is brighter than the inside, which is true during the day. At night, with the showroom lit, people outside can see in more easily.
- The holes reduce detail, so big images and bold text work far better than small print.
- It darkens the inside. In a small showroom, consider covering only part of the glass.

## Cut vinyl

Cut vinyl is solid-colour vinyl cut by machine into letters, logos and shapes. There is no background, so only the design sits on the glass.

**Use it for:** store name and logo on the door, timings, phone numbers, website and social handles, simple offer messages, and decorative lines or borders.

**Why people like it:** it looks crisp and clean, keeps most of the glass clear, and is easy to replace when timings or offers change. Fine details are possible, but very thin strokes are harder to apply and lift more easily, so keep small text to a sensible size.

## Printed vinyl and full-cover graphics

Sometimes you want a full, opaque graphic: covering a store under renovation, hiding a back wall from the street, or turning a side window into a display panel. Printed vinyl, laminated for durability, does this well. It blocks light completely, so use it only where that is what you want.

## What to check before installing

**1. Measure every pane separately.** Glass panels that look the same often differ slightly. Measure the visible area inside the frame, not the frame itself.

**2. Note the mullions and joins.** A design that runs across several panes should not put a face, a logo or a key word right on a metal divider.

**3. Check the glass.** Tinted, reflective or textured glass changes how graphics look and how well they stick. Old glass with scratches or paint marks needs cleaning first.

**4. Decide inside or outside.** Inside is usually the better choice. If you plan to fix graphics outside, the material and lamination need to suit sun and rain.

**5. Think about the view from inside.** Stand inside the showroom and imagine the graphic in place. Staff need to see the entrance and customers like to see the street.

**6. Check sunlight.** Glass that faces strong afternoon sun heats up. Ask for materials made for that, especially for full-cover vinyl.

**7. Ask about rules.** Some malls, buildings and market associations have guidelines for what can go on shop glass. Your landlord or mall management can tell you.

**8. Plan the installation time.** Glass work needs a clean, dry surface and some space to work. Installing before opening hours or after closing usually goes more smoothly.

## A simple combination that works

Many showrooms use all three together: a frosted band at eye level, cut vinyl for the name and timings on the door, and one-way vision on one large pane for the current campaign. When the campaign changes, only that one pane is reprinted.
