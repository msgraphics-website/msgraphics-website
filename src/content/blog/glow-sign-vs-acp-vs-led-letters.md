Most shop owners we meet already know they want a sign that is "big and bright". The harder question is which kind. In Delhi markets you will see three types again and again: the glow sign board, the ACP board, and LED letters. Each one is a good choice for some shops and a poor one for others.

Here is how they differ, in plain terms.

## Glow sign board

A glow sign board is a box. The frame is usually aluminium or MS (mild steel), the front is a printed backlit flex or vinyl, and inside are LED modules or tube lights. At night the whole face lights up evenly.

**Where it works well**

- Shops on busy roads where people see the sign from a distance
- Businesses that change offers or branding often, because the face can be reprinted
- Tight budgets, since it is usually the most economical lit option

**What to watch for**

- The printed face fades in strong sun. On a south or west facing front, expect to reprint sooner.
- Cheap frames rust at the joints after a couple of monsoons. Ask what the frame is made of.
- Uneven lighting (bright spots and dark corners) means the lights were spaced badly. A good board glows evenly.

## ACP board

ACP stands for aluminium composite panel: two thin aluminium sheets with a core in between. It comes in solid colours and finishes like matt, gloss and brushed metal. The panel is fixed on a frame, and your name goes on top as cut acrylic letters, steel letters or vinyl.

**Where it works well**

- Showrooms, clinics, offices and brands that want a clean, solid look
- Fronts that need to stay sharp for many years
- Buildings where the board also covers an old or uneven wall

**What to watch for**

- Changing the design later means new letters, not just a reprint
- The panel colour should match your brand colour closely. Ask to see a sample sheet.

## LED letters

LED letters (also called channel letters or 3D letters) are individual letters, each one lit from inside or from behind. They can be fixed on an ACP board, a glass front or straight on the wall.

**Where it works well**

- Premium stores and showrooms where the brand name is the hero
- Malls and high streets where many signs compete for attention
- Logos with clean, simple shapes

**What to watch for**

- Very thin fonts and small details are hard to build and do not light well
- Each letter has its own wiring, so installation needs more care
- Cost goes up with the number and height of letters

## A quick way to decide

| If you want… | Choose |
|---|---|
| The lowest cost for a lit sign | Glow sign board |
| A sign that still looks new in five years | ACP with acrylic or steel letters |
| The most premium look at night | LED letters |
| To change offers or branding every season | Glow sign board |
| A clean front for an office or clinic | ACP board |

Many shops combine them. A common setup is an ACP board across the full width with LED letters for the name and a small glow sign for the services or phone number.

## Things that affect the price, whatever you choose

- **Size**: measured in square feet for boards and in inches of height for letters
- **Height of installation**: a ground floor board is simpler than one on the third floor that needs scaffolding
- **Lighting**: LED modules cost more than tube lights but use less power and last longer
- **Material grade**: frame thickness, ACP thickness and acrylic quality all show up in the quote

When you compare quotes, ask each vendor to write these down. Two quotes for "10x4 glow sign board" can be very different boards.

## Before you order

1. **Measure the front**, or ask for a site survey. We measure before we fabricate, so the board fits the first time.
2. **Check the building rules.** Some markets, malls and RWAs have size or lighting limits. Your landlord or market association can tell you.
3. **Ask for a mock-up** of your shop front with the new sign. It is much easier to fix a colour or font on screen than on the wall.
4. **Confirm who installs it.** A sign made in one place and fitted by someone else is where most problems start.
