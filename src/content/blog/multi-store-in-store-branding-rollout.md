Branding one store is a design job. Branding fifty is a logistics job. The design can be perfect and the rollout can still fail because a window was a different size, a kit went to the wrong outlet, or the danglers in East Delhi came out a different red from the ones in Gurugram.

This is the checklist we use when a brand, distributor or franchise asks us to roll out in-store branding across many outlets.

## 1. Fix the list of stores first

Before any design work, get a clean list: store name, full address, contact person, phone number, and opening hours. Add a column for any landlord or mall rules.

It sounds basic. It is also the step that causes the most delays when it is skipped.

## 2. Survey before you size

Stores that look the same are rarely the same. One has a pillar in the middle of the window. Another has shelves 2 inches shorter. A third has a glass door that opens outward.

A quick survey of each outlet should record:

- Window and door glass sizes
- Shelf lengths and the height of the shelf edge (for shelf strips)
- Counter front size
- Ceiling height and fixing points (for danglers)
- Photos of each wall that will get branding

If a full survey is not possible, survey a sample of stores and group them into types: small, medium and large. Then design a kit for each type.

## 3. Make one master artwork

Create one master design for each item, then adapt it to each size. Do not let different vendors or designers recreate the artwork. Small changes add up, and by the tenth store the logo is in a slightly different place and the colours have drifted.

## 4. Approve a colour proof

Ask for a printed proof of the key items, on the actual material. A colour on screen and the same colour on flex, vinyl or sunboard can look quite different. We send a colour proof before every bulk run for this reason.

## 5. Pack kits store by store

Every outlet should get one packed kit, labelled with the store name and address, with a printed list of what is inside. The installer opens the box, checks the list, and knows exactly what goes where.

This alone saves most of the phone calls during a rollout.

## 6. Plan the installation route

Group stores by area and plan the routes so one crew can cover several outlets in a day. Confirm timings with each store in advance. Many shops prefer installation before opening time or during slow hours.

## 7. Get photo proof for every store

Ask for before and after photos of every outlet, ideally with the store name visible. This is your proof that the work was done and done right. It also helps your sales team check display compliance later.

We share a photo report for every installation as part of the job.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Guessing sizes** from one store and printing for all
- **Using different printers** for different regions, which leads to colour mismatch
- **Shipping loose items** instead of store-wise kits
- **No point of contact** at the store, so the crew waits outside
- **No photo record**, so nobody knows which stores are actually done

- Your artwork or brand guidelines
- The target date for the rollout to finish
