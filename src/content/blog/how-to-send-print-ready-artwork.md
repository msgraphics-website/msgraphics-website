Most printing delays are not caused by the printing. They are caused by the file, or by a quote that was based on the wrong details. A few minutes of checking at your end can save a day of back and forth.

## Part 1: The brief

When you ask for a quote, include these details. Without them, any price you get is a guess.

- **What it is**: visiting card, brochure, flyer, standee, sticker, box, flex banner
- **Size**: finished size, in mm, inches or feet
- **Quantity**: and whether you might need more later
- **Material**: paper GSM, or flex, vinyl, sunboard, acrylic
- **Printing**: one side or both sides, full colour or single colour
- **Finish**: matt or gloss lamination, spot UV, foil, die-cut shape, folding, binding
- **Delivery**: one address, or many
- **Deadline**: the date you need it in hand, not the date you hope to start

If you are not sure about GSM or finish, say what the item is for. "A brochure we hand out at a trade show" tells a printer a lot.

## Part 2: The file

### Size and bleed

Set the document to the exact finished size. Then add **3 mm bleed** on every side. Any background colour or image that touches the edge should extend into the bleed.

Keep important text and logos at least **3 to 5 mm inside** the cut line. That is the safe area.

### Colour mode: CMYK

Printing uses four inks: cyan, magenta, yellow and black. Design in CMYK from the start. If you design in RGB and convert at the end, some colours shift. Bright blues and greens are the usual victims.

If your brand has an exact colour, share its CMYK values or a Pantone reference, and ask for a printed proof before the full run.

### Resolution

- For brochures, cards and anything held in the hand: images at **300 dpi** at the final size
- For large banners and hoardings seen from a distance, lower resolution is fine. Ask your printer what they need for the size you are printing.

An image copied from WhatsApp or a website is usually too small, even if it looks fine on your phone.

### Fonts

Convert all text to outlines (curves), or send the font files along with the artwork. Otherwise the printer's computer replaces your font with another one, and the layout moves.

### Black text

Small black text should be **100% black (K) only**, not a mix of all four inks. Rich black on small text can look blurry if the inks do not line up perfectly.

### File format

- **PDF** (press quality) is the safest
- **CDR** and **AI** files are fine; include linked images
- Avoid sending Word files or screenshots for final printing

## Part 3: Before you say "go"

1. Read every word once more. Phone numbers and email addresses are where typos hide.
2. Check the proof on paper, not only on screen.
3. Confirm the quantity, size and finish in writing.
