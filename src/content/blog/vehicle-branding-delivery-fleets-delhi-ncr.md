A branded delivery van is on the road all day, through markets, residential colonies and office areas. For a business that runs a fleet in Delhi NCR, it is advertising that goes wherever your customers are. It also tells people your business is established, which matters when a driver turns up at someone's gate.

Doing it well means choosing the right coverage, the right vinyl and checking each vehicle properly before anything is applied.

## Full wrap or partial

**Full wrap.** Printed vinyl covers most of the painted body: sides, rear, bonnet and sometimes the roof. It gives the most impact and lets you change the vehicle's colour completely. It also needs the most material, the most time to apply and the most care around curves, handles and seams.

**Partial wrap.** Graphics cover selected areas, often the sides and rear doors, and the vehicle's own paint becomes part of the design. A white van with a strong band of colour, a large logo and clear contact details can look very smart.

**Cut vinyl lettering.** Just your logo, name and phone number, cut from coloured vinyl. Simple, quick and suitable for smaller vehicles or a first round of branding.

For many delivery fleets, a well-designed partial wrap gives most of the visibility of a full wrap. Choose a full wrap when the vehicle itself is a central part of your brand, or when the base paint colour does not suit your design.

## Design for a moving vehicle

People see your van at a signal, in traffic or parked outside a building. They have a few seconds.

- Make the brand name or logo large on both sides and the rear.
- Say what you do in a few words.
- Give one clear way to reach you, such as a phone number, a website or a WhatsApp number.
- Keep text away from door handles, panel gaps, fuel caps and windows where it will be cut.
- Remember the two sides of the vehicle are not mirror images. Doors, sliding panels and fuel caps sit in different places.

Ask for a design on the exact model and body type of your vehicle, not a generic van outline.

## Choosing the vinyl

Vehicle vinyl is not all the same. The two broad types are:

**Cast vinyl.** Thin and flexible, it stretches over curves, rivets and recesses and stays in place. It is the right choice for full wraps and complex body shapes.

**Calendered vinyl.** Thicker and less flexible. It works on flat panels and for cut lettering, and for short-term promotions, but tends to lift or shrink on deep curves over time.

Ask for:

- **Air-release vinyl**, which has tiny channels in the adhesive so bubbles can be pushed out during application
- **Lamination** over printed vinyl, to protect the print from Delhi sun, dust, rain and car washes
- **One-way vision** for rear and side windows if you want graphics across the glass while drivers can still see out. Check with your local traffic authority what is allowed on vehicle windows before you decide.

## What to check on each vehicle

Before branding, every vehicle should be inspected. Vinyl sticks to what is underneath it, so problems in the paint become problems in the wrap.

- **Paint condition.** Peeling, chipped or oxidised paint can lift with the vinyl, now or at removal.
- **Repainted panels.** Fresh or poor-quality repaint may not hold vinyl well.
- **Rust and dents.** Vinyl will not hide them, and rust can spread under it.
- **Cleanliness.** The surface must be washed, dried and free of wax and oil before application.
- **Model differences.** In a mixed fleet, two vehicles of the same make can have different panels. Measure each type.

## Planning a fleet rollout

Branding many vehicles needs coordination, because each one is off the road while it is being wrapped.

1. Agree the design on one vehicle first and check it in person.
2. Schedule vehicles in batches so deliveries are not disrupted.
3. Keep a simple record of which vehicle was branded when, so you can track wear later.
4. Plan how to handle new vehicles that join the fleet and old ones that leave.

## Looking after branded vehicles

Hand washing or gentle washing keeps the graphics in good shape. Avoid high-pressure jets close to the edges of the vinyl. Fix any lifting corner early, before dust and water get under it.

When the vehicle is sold or the branding changes, have the vinyl removed properly rather than peeled off in a hurry.
