Gurgaon is not one kind of retail location. A shop in an older sector market such as Sector 29 has very different needs from a unit in a busy high street of the Cyber Hub kind, a mall, or a showroom along Sohna Road or Golf Course Road. The sign that works in one place can look wrong, or simply not be allowed, in another.

This guide is for shop owners and marketing teams opening or refreshing a store in Gurgaon. It covers what to sort out before design, how to think about visibility, and when a lit sign is worth it.

## Start with the approvals, not the design

In Gurgaon, a lot of retail space is managed. Malls, high-street complexes and commercial buildings usually have their own fit-out teams and a signage guideline. That guideline may cover:

- The maximum size and position of your fascia sign
- Whether signs can be lit, and how (face-lit, halo-lit, or not at all)
- Approved materials and fixing methods
- Working hours for installation and the paperwork needed to bring a team in

Ask your landlord or the mall management for this before you brief a designer. It saves you from paying for a design that has to be redone. For standalone shops and showrooms on main roads, check with your landlord, the market association or the local municipal body about what is allowed on the facade.

Once you have the guideline, share it with your sign maker. We work from it directly and send drawings in the format the mall team asks for.

## Mall and high-street units

Inside malls and covered high streets, people walk past at close range. The sign does not need to shout. It needs to look clean, match your brand colours exactly, and be readable from the corridor and from the opposite side.

What usually works:

- **LED letters on an ACP or painted backing.** Neat, premium and easy to approve.
- **Halo-lit letters**, where light glows from behind onto the wall. It looks refined and often fits mall guidelines that limit brightness.
- **A projecting or blade sign**, if the guideline allows one, so shoppers walking along the corridor can see you before they reach your front.

Mall installations normally happen at night or before opening hours. Plan for it, and make sure your sign maker can work in that window.

## Open-air high streets and sector markets

Places like Sector 29 and similar open-air retail strips have a mix of walking traffic and cars. Signs are seen from closer than on a highway, but they face sun, dust and rain.

Here, ACP boards with acrylic or LED letters give a long-lasting look. A glow sign board is a practical choice for restaurants, cafes and shops that change their offers often, because the face can be reprinted. If your unit is in a row of restaurants, everyone's sign competes at night, so strong contrast and a simple name matter more than extra decoration.

## Main roads: Sohna Road, Golf Course Road and similar

On wide, fast roads, most people see your sign from a moving car. They have a couple of seconds at most.

- Keep the name large and the message short.
- Think about angles. A sign parallel to the road is hard to read from a car. A side-facing sign or a pylon near the entrance can help, where permitted.
- Make sure it is visible past trees, service lanes and parked cars. Stand on the road and look before you decide the height.
- Lit signs are almost always worth it here, because a lot of the traffic passes in the evening.

## Lit or non-lit

A quick way to decide:

| Location | Usually works |
|---|---|
| Inside a mall | LED or halo-lit letters, as per mall guidelines |
| Covered high street | LED letters on ACP, or a slim glow sign |
| Open-air sector market | ACP with LED letters, or a glow sign board |
| Main road showroom | Large LED letters or a glow sign, plus a pylon if allowed |
| Office-style frontage | Non-lit steel or acrylic letters can be enough |

Non-lit signs are cheaper to run and simpler to install, but they disappear after dark. If your business depends on evening footfall, budget for lighting.

## Practical points for Gurgaon installations

- **Power and timers.** Confirm where the sign will draw power from and who maintains it in a managed building.
- **Height and access.** Upper-floor fascias need scaffolding or a lift. Some buildings require a safety plan for this.
- **Dust.** Gurgaon roads can be dusty, especially near construction. Sealed letters and easy-to-clean faces make maintenance simpler.
- **Matching across stores.** If you have outlets in Delhi and Gurgaon, use the same specification so every store looks the same.
