Every brand wants its name on the kirana counter and the supermarket shelf. The shopkeeper, however, has limited space and a lot of people asking for it. Material that blocks the shelves, peels in a week or covers the shop's own name gets taken down, quietly, as soon as the merchandiser leaves.

The branding that stays up is the branding that works for the store as well as for the brand. Here is how to plan it.

## Start from the shopkeeper's side

Before deciding what to make, look at the store from behind the counter. A kirana owner in Uttam Nagar or Palam cares about a few practical things:

- Will it take up space I need for stock?
- Will it make it harder for me or my customers to reach products?
- Will it look neat for months, or become something I have to clean or throw away?
- Does it help my shop in some way?

If the answers are good, the branding will usually be accepted and kept. If not, no amount of design will save it.

## Counter branding

The counter is where every customer stands, so it is the most valuable spot in a small store.

**What tends to work**

- **Counter front panels** on sunboard, ACP or printed vinyl, covering the front face of the counter. It hides an old or damaged counter, which shopkeepers often appreciate.
- **Counter top units** for small, impulse items. They should be compact and placed where they do not block the billing area.
- **Change mats and small trays** printed with the brand.
- **Tent cards and small acrylic stands** for offers and new launches.

**What to avoid:** large counter units that eat into the billing space, and anything that covers the shop's weighing scale, card machine or cash drawer.

## Shelf branding

On shelves, the aim is to make your products easy to find without hiding anyone else's.

- **Shelf strips** along the front edge turn your row into a recognisable block. Make sure they fit the actual shelf height and leave room for price labels.
- **Wobblers** draw attention to a new product or offer. Keep them small so they do not cover the shelf below.
- **Branded shelf units or racks** given to the store are popular, because they add display space. They need to fit the floor area and be sturdy enough for daily use.
- **Fridge and chiller stickers** for beverages and dairy, fixed on the side or door without blocking the view inside.

## Gondola branding in supermarkets

Supermarkets in places like Dwarka, Janakpuri, Gurgaon and Noida usually have gondolas: double-sided shelf units in the middle of the floor.

A typical gondola branding set includes:

1. **Header board** on top, visible from across the store
2. **End cap or side panel** at the aisle end, the most valuable face of the gondola
3. **Shelf strips** along each shelf edge
4. **Base or kick plate** branding near the floor
5. **Floor graphics** in front of the section, with anti-slip lamination

Supermarket chains often have their own rules for in-store branding, sizes and installation timing. Always check with the store manager or the chain's head office before you fabricate anything.

## Outside the store

Many kirana stores accept branding outside the shop more easily than inside, because it does not take any shelf space.

- **Shop sign boards** with the shop's name and the brand's logo
- **Shutter branding**, which is seen after closing time
- **Pillar and wall wraps** near the entrance

Any branding on the shop front can be affected by building, market or local rules. Ask the shop owner to confirm with the landlord or market association before installation.

## Material choices that last

Small stores are tough environments: dust, oil, moisture, constant handling and cleaning.

- Use **laminated vinyl** or **PVC** for anything that will be wiped.
- Choose **sunboard or ACP** for panels that need to stay flat.
- Avoid thin paper for anything meant to last more than a short promotion.
- Round off sharp corners on anything at hand height.

## Measure before you print

Shelves, counters and gondolas vary a lot between stores, even in the same market. A quick survey with photos and measurements of each store saves a lot of wasted material. For a large rollout, survey a sample of stores, group them into a few standard kit sizes and keep a small set of custom sizes for the rest.

## Installation and follow-up

Branding that is left at the store "to be fixed later" is rarely fixed. Plan for a team to install it, take a photo of each completed store and note any store that refused or needed a change. That record is useful when you plan the next round.
