POP (point of purchase) material is meant to do one job: make a shopper notice a product at the moment they are close enough to buy it. When it works, it is the cheapest salesman in the store. When it does not, it ends up behind the counter or in the bin within a week.

The difference is usually not the design. It is putting the right piece in the right place, made from a material that survives the store.

## Standees

A standee stands on the floor, usually near the entrance or at the end of an aisle. It is the largest POP piece and is seen from furthest away.

**Types you will come across**

- **Roll-up standee**: the print rolls into an aluminium base. Quick to set up, easy to move between stores and events.
- **X-banner**: a light frame with the print held at four corners. Economical for short promotions.
- **Cut-out standee**: printed and cut to the shape of a person, product or mascot, mounted on sunboard or foam board with a support at the back.

**Where it works:** entrances, near the billing counter, at the start of an aisle, in front of a promotional stack.

**What to check:** a standee needs floor space, which is exactly what small stores in Lajpat Nagar or Karol Bagh do not have. Keep the base footprint small and ask the store where it can stand before you send one.

## Danglers

A dangler hangs from the ceiling, a rail or the top of a shelf. It is printed on both sides because people see it from two directions as they walk.

**Where it works:** over an aisle, above a product section, in front of a fridge or chiller, in stores with high ceilings.

**What to check:**

- Hanging height. Too low and staff and customers knock it down. Too high and nobody reads it.
- Material. Thin paper curls under fans and air-conditioning. A stiffer board or sunboard hangs flatter.
- How it will be fixed. Ask what the store ceiling or rail allows. Many stores do not want anything taped or tied to their false ceiling.

## Wobblers

A wobbler is a small printed card, often cut to a shape, fixed to the shelf edge on a flexible plastic strip. Every time someone walks past, it moves slightly.

**Where it works:** right where the product sits, especially for new launches, offers and products that are easy to miss in a crowded shelf.

**What to check:** keep them small and light. A large wobbler blocks the products on the shelf below, which is the fastest way to have it removed. The fixing tape should be strong enough to stay but should not damage the shelf.

## Shelf strips (shelf talkers)

A shelf strip runs along the front edge of the shelf, usually in the price channel or stuck to the shelf lip. It turns a whole shelf into a branded block.

**Where it works:** gondolas and wall shelves in supermarkets, chemists and kirana stores where your products occupy a full row.

**What to check:**

- Measure the shelf edge height. Shelf channels vary between store fixtures.
- Leave space for the store's own price labels, or design around them.
- Use a tough, wipeable material such as PVC or laminated card, since shelves get cleaned often.

## Other pieces that often go with them

- **Tent cards** on counters and billing desks
- **Counter tops and small acrylic stands** near the cash point
- **Floor graphics** with anti-slip lamination, to guide shoppers to a section
- **Fridge and chiller stickers** on the door or side panel
- **Header boards** fixed on top of a gondola

## A simple way to plan a POP kit

1. **Visit or photograph a few typical stores.** A large supermarket in Gurgaon and a small store in Uttam Nagar need very different kits.
2. **Pick one hero piece and two or three support pieces.** For example, a standee at the entrance, wobblers at the shelf and a tent card at the counter.
3. **Match the material to how long it needs to last.** A weekend offer can use lighter material. A three-month campaign needs sturdier board and lamination.
4. **Pack by store, not by item.** Each store should receive one labelled pack with everything it needs and a photo showing where each piece goes.
5. **Plan who installs it.** POP left with the store to "put up later" often never goes up.

## Why some POP never gets used

Most wasted POSM comes from three things: pieces that are too big for real stores, materials that look tired after a few days, and kits that arrive without anyone to install them. Testing a sample kit in a couple of stores before the full run solves most of this.
