A visiting card is the cheapest piece of branding most businesses own, and also the one people judge most quickly. Someone takes it, feels it between two fingers, and forms an opinion before they read your name. Thin paper, a blurry logo or a lamination that peels at the corner all say something about the business.

The good news is that a better card does not need to be complicated. It comes down to three choices: paper, finish and quantity.

## Paper and GSM

GSM means grams per square metre. In simple words, the higher the number, the thicker and stiffer the card.

- **Around 250 to 300 GSM**: acceptable for budget runs and internal staff, but it feels light and bends easily.
- **300 to 350 GSM**: the sweet spot for most shops, clinics, offices and traders. It feels like a proper card.
- **400 GSM and above**: thick and premium. Good for designers, architects, consultants and anyone whose card is part of their pitch.

You can also choose textured or coloured papers, such as linen-finish, kraft or pearl boards. These look different but take ink differently, so fine text and light colours need care. Ask to see a printed sample on the actual paper before you commit.

## Lamination and finishes

Lamination is a thin film applied over the printed card. It protects the print and changes how the card looks and feels.

**Gloss lamination** makes colours look bright and punchy. It shows fingerprints and reflects light, so it suits photo-heavy or very colourful designs more than minimal ones.

**Matt lamination** removes the shine. Text is easy to read under any light, and the card looks calm and professional. This is the most common choice we see for offices and showrooms.

**Velvet or soft-touch lamination** feels smooth, almost like suede. It is the finish people comment on when they pick the card up. It pairs well with dark backgrounds and simple layouts.

**Spot UV** is a clear, glossy coating applied only to certain parts of the card, usually the logo or name, on top of matt or velvet lamination. The contrast between shiny and non-shiny catches the light. It works best on bold shapes. Very thin lines and small text do not show the effect well.

A few other options are worth knowing about:

- **Foil stamping** in gold, silver or other metallic shades for a logo or border
- **Rounded corners**, which also stop corners from fraying in a wallet
- **Embossing or debossing**, where part of the card is raised or pressed in
- **Unlaminated cards**, if people need to write on them, for example appointment cards for a clinic

One practical point: if your team often writes a mobile number or a note on the back, keep the back unlaminated or use matt. Gloss and velvet are hard to write on with a normal ballpoint pen.

## Single side or both sides

Printing on both sides costs a little more but gives you space to breathe. A common layout is the logo alone on one side and contact details on the other. If you have a lot to say, such as a list of services or branch addresses in Janakpuri, Rajouri Garden and Gurgaon, the back side keeps the front from looking crowded.

A QR code that opens your WhatsApp, website or Google Maps location is now normal on Delhi visiting cards. Test it on two or three phones before printing the full run.

## How many to order

This is where people often waste money. Ordering in large numbers lowers the cost per card, but only if you actually use them before something changes.

Before you decide, think about:

1. **How many people you meet each week.** A field sales executive covering Uttam Nagar, Palam and Dwarka will use far more cards than an accounts manager.
2. **What might change soon.** A new phone number, a shift to a new office, a promotion or a rebrand will make the old stock useless.
3. **How many people need cards.** For a team, it is usually better to order a moderate quantity for everyone at the same time, with the same finish, so the cards match.

For premium finishes like velvet, foil or spot UV, order slightly fewer and reprint when needed. For a simple matt card that will be handed out at a counter all day, a larger quantity makes sense.

## Getting the artwork right

Most visiting card problems are artwork problems, not printing problems.

- Keep important text away from the edges. Cards are cut in stacks, and a small shift is normal.
- Add bleed if your background colour runs to the edge.
- Avoid very small text, especially in light grey on white.
- Check every phone number and email twice. This is the most common reprint reason.
