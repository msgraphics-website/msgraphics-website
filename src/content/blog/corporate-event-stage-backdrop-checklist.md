A corporate event is remembered through its photographs, and almost every photograph has the stage in the background. The backdrop, the podium and the standees at the entrance carry your brand long after the evening is over. They also have to go up in a few hours in a hall that someone else was using the night before.

This checklist covers what to decide, what to ask the venue and what to keep ready.

## Start with the venue details

Before anyone opens a design file, collect these from the venue:

- Stage width, depth and height from the floor
- Ceiling height, or truss height if there is a truss
- If you can use the venue's truss or need your own frame
- Where the screen or LED wall sits, if there is one
- Entry route for material, lift size and loading point
- Setup and teardown timings

Hotels in Aerocity and Gurgaon, banquets in South Delhi and convention halls in Dwarka and Pragati Maidan all work differently. A five-minute site visit, or even good photos and a measured sketch from the venue team, saves hours of reprinting later.

## Backdrop sizes and layout

Size the backdrop to the stage, not to a standard number. A backdrop that is too small looks lost on a wide stage. One that is too tall gets cut off by a low ceiling or hidden behind lights.

Some layout points that matter on the day:

- **Keep the logo and event name in the upper half.** The lower part is blocked by the podium, the panel table and the people standing on stage.
- **Leave space for the screen.** If there is an LED wall in the middle, design the backdrop as two side panels and a header, not one big graphic.
- **Think about photographs.** The logo should appear in a group photo taken from the floor, so repeat it if the stage is wide.
- **Large text, less of it.** Event name, date, logo and maybe a tagline. Long sentences do not read from the back of a hall.

## Flex or fabric

**Flex** is quick to print, low on effort and fine for one-day events, outdoor stages and anything that will be thrown away after. It can show creases and reflects stage lights, which some photographers dislike.

**Fabric**, usually printed by sublimation and fitted on a frame, gives a smooth, matte finish with no glare. It folds into a bag and can be reused, which helps if the same backdrop travels to events in Noida, Gurgaon and other cities through the year.

For awards nights, launches and conferences with heavy photography, fabric is usually worth it. For a quick dealer meet or a short outdoor event, flex does the job.

Ask your venue whether they need fire-retardant material. Many hotels and convention centres do, and it should be specified before printing, not after.

## The rest of the branding

The stage is the centre, but guests see a lot before they reach it:

- **Entrance arch or welcome board** with the event name
- **Standees** at the entrance, registration desk and lift lobby
- **Registration desk front panel**
- **Podium panel** with the logo, sized to the venue's podium or your own
- **Directional signage** if the hall is far from the lobby
- **Photo wall or selfie booth** with a repeat logo pattern
- **Table tent cards and name cards** for panel discussions
- **Lanyards and badges**, if not already handled

Make a list with quantities and sizes, and share it with the venue so they know what to expect.

## Setup and teardown

This is where most stress comes from. Venues often release the hall late, and you may get only a few hours before guests arrive.

- Get the setup window and teardown deadline in writing
- Pre-fit the backdrop frame and panels at the workshop, so on site it is assembly only
- Label every piece by position (left panel, right panel, header, podium)
- Keep a small kit: tape, cable ties, spare clips, a few spare standee bases
- Plan teardown with the same crew, since venues usually want the hall empty by a fixed time

## Venue rules to check

Every venue has its own rules, and they change. Check with the venue's banquet or events team on:

- Fixing on walls, pillars and ceilings (most venues do not allow nails or tape on their surfaces)
- Fire-retardant material requirements
- Electrical load and who handles power connections
- Vendor entry passes and ID requirements
- Any charges for using the service lift or loading area

## A short checklist

- Venue measurements and photos received
- Backdrop layout approved, with screen position marked
- Material confirmed: flex or fabric, fire-retardant if required
- Full list of standees, panels and signage with sizes
- Setup and teardown times confirmed in writing
- Crew, transport and vendor passes arranged
- Spare kit packed
