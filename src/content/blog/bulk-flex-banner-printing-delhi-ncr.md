A single banner is easy. Two hundred banners going to different shops, booths or polling areas across Delhi NCR is a small logistics project. Most problems with bulk flex jobs are not about print quality. They are about the wrong size for a location, banners that tear in the first strong wind, or bundles that arrive without anyone knowing which one goes where.

Here is how to plan a bulk run so it goes up properly.

## Fix the sizes first

Before design starts, list every place a banner will go and measure it, or ask someone on site to measure it. Then group them into as few sizes as possible.

Common formats include:

- **Horizontal shop and road banners**, wide and short, for shop fronts, railings and boundary walls
- **Vertical pole banners**, tall and narrow, often printed in pairs for both sides of a pole
- **Backdrops** for stages, press meets and event entrances
- **Square or near-square panels** for frames and boards

Fewer sizes means fewer design versions, faster printing and less confusion during installation. If a location is slightly different, it is usually easier to adjust the fixing than to create a one-off size.

Measure in feet and inches and note whether the size is the visible area or the full banner including edges. This small detail causes a lot of reprints.

## Eyelets, pockets or pasting

How the banner is fixed decides how it is finished.

**Eyelets** are metal rings punched along the edges. The banner is tied with rope or cable ties. This is the usual method for railings, poles, scaffolding and temporary frames. For outdoor use, ask for the edge to be folded and welded or stitched before the eyelets go in. A single layer of flex with eyelets tends to tear at the corners.

**Pole pockets** are sleeves at the top and bottom for a pipe or rod. They keep vertical banners straight and tidy.

**Pasting** means fixing the flex flat on a wooden frame, board or wall. It suits semi-permanent shop banners and boards where you want a smooth, tight surface.

**Wind slits** are small cuts that let air pass through large banners. They help on open sites and high locations, although the graphic loses a little at each cut.

## Outdoor or indoor material

**Outdoor banners** are usually printed on frontlit flex with solvent inks. The material is heavier and handles sun, rain and dust. For anything that will stay up for weeks in a Delhi summer or monsoon, this is the right choice.

**Indoor banners** for halls, malls, showrooms and offices look better on a lighter flex, a fabric or a canvas, printed with eco-solvent inks for finer detail. People see them from a few feet away, so sharpness matters more than toughness.

**Backlit** banners are for light boxes and glow signs only. Using frontlit flex there makes the colours look dull at night.

If you are not sure, tell your printer how long the banner needs to last and where it will hang. Our flex, vinyl and one-way vision guide covers the materials in more detail.

## Working out quantities

The simplest way is a location sheet. One row per location, with columns for:

1. Area or address, for example a market in Janakpuri or a ward in Uttam Nagar
2. Size and orientation
3. Fixing method
4. Number of banners at that spot
5. Contact person for installation

Add up each size from the sheet. Then add a small spare quantity for each size, because some banners get torn, stolen or damaged during fixing. For long campaigns, plan a replacement run in advance rather than scrambling later.

If different areas need different text, such as a local phone number, branch address or candidate name, mark it in the sheet. Variable details are easy to print once the list is clear. They are hard to fix after the bundles are packed.

## Shops, events and elections

**Shops** usually need a few banners that last a full season. Choose good material and proper finishing, and keep the design simple enough to read from across the road.

**Events** need banners that look sharp in photos. Check the stage and entry sizes with the venue and confirm when you can enter to install.

**Election and campaign banners** are time-bound and high-volume. Content approval, permissions and the places you are allowed to put banners can all be governed by rules that change. Check with the relevant local authority and election office before you print and install.

For any outdoor banner on public property, confirm permission first. Your market association, RWA or local authority can tell you what is allowed.

## Packing and delivery

Ask for banners to be rolled or folded by location, with a label on each bundle. When the run is going to Dwarka, Palam, Noida and Faridabad on the same day, clear labelling saves hours on the ground.
