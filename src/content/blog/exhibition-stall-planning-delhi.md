Delhi hosts trade shows almost every month, at venues like Bharat Mandapam at Pragati Maidan, Yashobhoomi (IICC) in Dwarka and India Expo Mart in Greater Noida. Most exhibitors book the space early and then leave the stall for later. That is where the stress starts.

Here is a simple timeline that keeps the stall on track.

## As soon as you book the space

**Find out what you have booked.** There are two common types:

- **Shell scheme**: the organiser gives you basic walls, a fascia name board, some lights and furniture. You add graphics and maybe a counter.
- **Raw or bare space**: you get only the floor. Everything, from flooring to walls to lights, is built by your fabricator.

**Download the exhibitor manual.** It has the rules you must follow: maximum height, fire safety requirements, electrical load, deadlines for drawing approval, and build-up and dismantling timings. Share it with your stall partner on day one.

## Design stage

A good stall brief answers these questions:

- What is the one thing visitors should remember about you?
- Which products will be displayed, and how big are they?
- Do you need a meeting area, a storage room or a product demo table?
- How many staff will stand at the stall?
- Is this a one-time stall or something you want to reuse?

Your stall partner should come back with a 3D view and a floor plan. Check it from the visitor's side of the aisle. Can people see your name and what you do from a few steps away?

## Approval stage

For raw space, most organisers want stall drawings, and sometimes structural and electrical details, before they allow you to build. Missing this deadline can mean fines or delays on build-up day, so plan for it.

## Fabrication and printing

This is where an in-house unit helps. The structure, the printed panels, the backlit graphics and the counter can all be made and checked in one place before they reach the venue.

Ask for:

- A colour proof of the main graphics
- Fire-retardant materials where the organiser requires them
- A packing list, so nothing is left behind at the workshop

## Build-up day

Build-up time at the venue is short, and every exhibitor is working at once. A smooth build-up depends on what was done before it:

- Panels pre-cut and pre-printed, not cut on site
- Electrical points planned in the drawing
- A crew that knows the layout and has assembled the stall before, even partly

## During and after the show

Keep a small kit at the stall: spare vinyl, tape, cable ties, a few extra brochures. Then plan dismantling. If the stall is reusable, label and pack the panels properly so they survive storage.

## A rough timeline

| When | What |
|---|---|
| At booking | Read the exhibitor manual, fix the budget |
| Next | Brief, 3D design, revisions |
| Before the organiser's deadline | Submit drawings for approval |
| After approval | Fabrication, printing, colour proofs |
| Just before the show | Trial fit, packing, transport |
| Build-up day | Installation and final checks |

The exact dates depend on your show, so work backwards from the organiser's deadlines.
