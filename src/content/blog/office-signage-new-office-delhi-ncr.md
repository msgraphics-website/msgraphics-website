Moving into a new office involves a long list of jobs, and signage often gets pushed to the last week. That is when people discover that the reception wall has no power point for a lit logo, the cabin names have changed, or the glass partitions need frosting before anyone can use the meeting rooms.

Planning signage alongside the fit-out avoids most of this. Here is a checklist for an office in Delhi NCR, from the building entrance to the last cabin.

## 1. Building and entrance signage

If you are in a commercial tower or business park in Gurgaon, Noida or Delhi, the building will usually have a lobby directory and rules about what tenants can put up outside their office door. Ask the building management for their signage guideline early. It often specifies sizes, materials and where your name can appear.

For a standalone office or a ground floor unit, you may want an outdoor sign as well. Check with your landlord and the local municipal body about facade signage before you design it.

## 2. Reception logo

The reception logo is the sign most visitors remember. Common options:

- **Acrylic letters**, cut and fixed on the wall or on a backing panel. Clean and versatile.
- **Brushed steel or brass-finish letters** for a more formal look.
- **Halo-lit letters**, where light glows from behind onto the wall. They look premium but need a power point and space behind the letters for wiring.
- **A logo on an ACP or wooden backing panel**, useful when the wall itself is uneven or has a textured finish.

Decide this before the reception wall is finished. If the logo is lit, the electrician needs to leave a concealed power point in the right place. Ask for a scaled mock-up of the logo on a photo of the wall so you can judge the size.

## 3. Floor directories and wayfinding

In offices spread across a large floor or several floors, people need help finding their way. Plan for:

- A directory near the lift lobby or entrance showing departments, meeting rooms and facilities
- Directional signs at corridor junctions
- Signs for washrooms, pantry, server room and stores

Keep the wording short and the style consistent. Use the same font, colours and arrow style throughout. Name meeting rooms in a way that is easy to say and easy to sign.

## 4. Door plates and cabin names

Door and cabin plates are small, but there are many of them, so the details matter:

- **Material:** acrylic, aluminium or a combination. Slide-in designs let you change names without replacing the whole plate.
- **Fixing:** double-sided tape on glass, screws on wood. Confirm what each door is made of.
- **Content:** decide whether plates carry a person's name, a designation or only a room number. Names change more often than room numbers.

Make one final list of every door and cabin with the exact wording, and get it approved by one person before production.

## 5. Glass manifestation and frosting

Most new offices in NCR use a lot of glass. Glass manifestation, a band of frosted or printed vinyl at eye level, has two jobs: making the glass visible so people do not walk into it, and adding privacy or branding.

Options include:

- Plain frosted vinyl bands
- Frosted vinyl with your logo or pattern cut out
- Printed graphics on clear or frosted film
- Full-height frosting with a clear strip, for meeting rooms that need privacy

Glass work should be measured after the glazing is installed, not from drawings. Small differences in the panel sizes are common.

## 6. Safety and statutory signs

Every office needs safety signage, such as fire exit signs, fire extinguisher location signs, assembly point signs and emergency information. Many of these are better in photoluminescent (glow-in-the-dark) material, so they remain visible if the power goes out.

The exact requirements depend on your building and the type of office, so check with your building management, your fire safety consultant or the relevant local authority. Your sign maker can produce the signs to the specification they give you.

## 7. Branded walls and graphics

Beyond the signs themselves, many offices add wall graphics: a brand story in the reception, values on a meeting room wall or a large printed mural in the work area. These are usually printed on vinyl or wallpaper and applied after painting is complete.

## Putting it together

A simple order of work helps:

1. Get the building guideline and the final layout.
2. Make a signage schedule: every sign, its location, size, material and wording.
3. Approve designs and samples.
4. Coordinate power points and wall finishes with the interior team.
5. Produce, then install in one or two planned visits near the end of the fit-out.
