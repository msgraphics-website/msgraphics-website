"Print it on flex" is often the first thing people say when they need anything outdoors. Flex is a good material, but it is not always the right one. Choosing the wrong one usually shows up later as a print that fades, peels, or simply looks cheap up close.

Here are the materials we print most often, and where each one belongs.

## Frontlit flex

This is the standard banner material. It is a PVC sheet, printed on one side, and either stretched on a frame or tied with eyelets.

**Use it for:** hoardings, unipoles, building wraps, construction-site boards, event backdrops, shop banners.

**Good to know:**

- It is lit from the front, by sun or by spotlights. Light from behind will look patchy.
- For large hoardings viewed from far away, the print does not need very high resolution. For a banner people walk past, it does.
- Ask for proper eyelets and hemmed edges on banners that will face wind. Torn corners are the most common failure.

## Backlit flex

Backlit flex looks similar but is made to let light pass through evenly.

**Use it for:** glow sign boards, light boxes, backlit standees.

**Good to know:** If frontlit flex is used in a glow sign, the colours look dull at night and you can see the tube lights through it. The material matters as much as the print.

## Vinyl

Vinyl is a thin self-adhesive film. It is printed and then stuck onto a surface.

**Use it for:** glass doors and windows, sunboard and ACP panels, walls, counters, floors (with a special anti-slip lamination), and vehicles.

**Good to know:**

- Outdoors, laminated vinyl lasts much longer than unlaminated vinyl. The lamination protects the ink from sun and scratches.
- Vehicle branding needs a vinyl that can stretch over curves. Ask for it specifically.
- Surface preparation matters. Vinyl on a dusty or oily wall will lift at the edges.

## One-way vision

One-way vision is a vinyl full of tiny holes. From outside you see the graphic. From inside, people can still look out.

**Use it for:** shop windows, office glass, bus and car rear windows.

**Good to know:** The holes reduce how solid the print looks, so bold designs with large shapes work better than fine text. It also lets in less daylight, which is worth thinking about for small shops.

## Canvas and fabric

Canvas and fabric prints have a softer, more premium look than flex.

**Use it for:** indoor displays, office walls, exhibitions, retail backdrops.

## Eco-solvent or solvent printing?

You may hear both terms. In simple words:

- **Solvent** printing is common for big outdoor jobs like hoardings, where the print is seen from far away.
- **Eco-solvent** printing gives finer detail and less smell, so it suits prints seen up close: shop graphics, standees, indoor branding and vehicle vinyl.

A good printer picks the machine to match the job. That is one reason we keep both large-format and eco-solvent/UV printing in-house.

## Five questions to ask before you print outdoors

1. Will it be lit, and from the front or the back?
2. How long does it need to last: one event, one season, or a year or more?
3. Will it be stretched on a frame, tied with ropes, or stuck to a surface?
4. How far away will people be when they see it?
5. Does it face direct afternoon sun?

If you share these answers with your printer, you will get a quote for the right material instead of the cheapest one.
