A hoarding campaign looks simple from the road: a big print on a big frame. Behind it is a chain of decisions about where, how big, what material and when. Getting any one of them wrong can mean a great design in the wrong place, or a launch date missed by a week.

This is a practical walk-through for marketing managers and business owners planning hoardings in Delhi NCR.

## Step 1: Decide what the campaign is for

Be clear about the goal before you look at sites. A new store opening needs hoardings close to the store, on the routes people use to reach it. A real estate project needs visibility on the roads its buyers travel. A brand awareness campaign might spread across several parts of the city.

The goal decides how many sites you need, where they are and how long they need to run.

## Step 2: Choose locations

In Delhi NCR, traffic patterns differ a lot between areas. A few things to think about:

- **Where your audience travels.** Office-goers in Gurgaon, families in Dwarka or Rohini, and shoppers in South Delhi markets use different roads.
- **Speed of traffic.** On expressways and flyovers, people see a hoarding for a second or two. In slow-moving traffic near markets and signals, they look for longer.
- **Angle and clutter.** A hoarding hidden behind trees, metro pillars or a row of other hoardings is worth less than it looks on a map. Visit the site, or ask for current photos taken from the road.
- **Lighting.** If most of your audience passes in the evening, a lit site matters.

Hoarding sites are usually owned or managed by media owners, building owners or agencies. Permissions for outdoor advertising are handled through the local municipal body or the site owner, so confirm with them that the site is legally available for the dates you need.

## Step 3: Fix the sizes

Hoardings come in many sizes, and the same campaign often runs on several formats: large roadside hoardings, unipoles, building wraps and smaller pole kiosks.

Ask the site owner for the exact visible size of each site and the bleed they need. Do not rely on rounded numbers. A design made for one shape and stretched to fit another always shows.

## Step 4: Design for the road

Hoarding design is different from print or digital design:

- One message, not three
- Large, simple text: a headline of a few words
- A strong image or product shot
- Your brand name and one way to reach you, such as a website or phone number
- High contrast, so it reads in harsh sun and under spotlights

Make master artwork, then adapt it carefully for each size rather than squashing one layout into every frame.

## Step 5: Pick the material

**Frontlit flex** is the standard choice for hoardings and unipoles. It is printed, then stretched and tied or clamped onto the frame, and lit from the front by sun or spotlights.

**Backlit flex** is for sites lit from behind.

**Vinyl** is used where the graphic is stuck directly onto a surface, such as a wall, a glass facade or a smaller board, rather than stretched on a frame.

For very large prints seen from far away, a solvent print on flex is normal. For smaller formats seen close up, finer printing is worth it. Ask your printer which machine and material they plan to use.

## Step 6: Printing and colour

Large prints are made in panels and joined, so ask how joins are handled and where they will fall. Avoid joins across faces or key text.

Before the full run, approve a colour proof. Brand colours shift between screen and flex, and it is far easier to adjust before printing than after mounting.

## Step 7: Mounting

Mounting is skilled and physical work at height. A good team will:

- Remove the old print cleanly
- Check the frame for damage or loose sections
- Stretch the new flex tight so it does not sag or flap in wind
- Secure the edges properly, because loose corners tear first
- Take photos after mounting as proof for your records

Plan mounting for times when traffic is lower, and allow a buffer for weather. Strong winds and heavy rain can delay work at height.

## Lead times to allow for

Every campaign is different, but work backwards from your go-live date and allow time for:

1. Site selection and booking
2. Design and approvals, including any internal sign-offs
3. Colour proof and final artwork
4. Printing and finishing
5. Mounting across all sites
6. A small buffer for weather or late changes

Festive seasons are busy for both sites and printers, so start earlier around those dates.
