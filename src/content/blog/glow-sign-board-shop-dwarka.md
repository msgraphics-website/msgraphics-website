## What a glow sign board actually is

A glow sign board is a box with a printed face and lights inside. The frame is usually aluminium or MS (mild steel). The face is backlit flex or translucent vinyl on acrylic. Inside, LED modules are fixed in rows so the light spreads across the whole face.

When it is switched on, the face should glow evenly from corner to corner. If you can see bright spots, dark corners or the outline of the lights, the board was not built well.

## Choosing the right size

The size is set by your shop front, not by a standard chart. A few things decide it:

- **The width of your shutter or front.** Most boards run the full width of the shop, sometimes with a small overlap on each side.
- **The height available above the shutter.** In many Dwarka sector markets the space above the shutter is limited by the floor above, a balcony or a neighbour's board.
- **How far away people will read it.** A board on a main road near Dwarka Mor or the Palam side is read from moving traffic, so the name needs to be large and simple. A board inside a pocket market can carry a little more detail.
- **Market or building rules.** Some markets and building societies prefer boards of a similar height so the row looks neat. Check with your landlord or market association before you fix a size.

We always measure the front before we fabricate. A board that is a few inches too wide or too tall is a common and avoidable problem.

## Frame and face materials

**Frame.** Aluminium frames are lighter and do not rust, which helps through Delhi monsoons. MS frames are strong and economical but need proper painting and sealing at the joints, or rust shows up after a season or two. Ask which one you are getting and how the joints are finished.

**Face.** For most shops, backlit flex is the practical choice. It is printed on a large-format machine, stretched on the frame and can be reprinted later if your offer or branding changes. Translucent vinyl on acrylic gives a cleaner, more solid look and suits smaller boards and premium fronts.

Make sure the face material is meant for backlighting. Frontlit flex inside a glow sign looks dull at night and shows the lights through it.

## Lighting

Most glow sign boards today use LED modules rather than tube lights. LEDs use less power, run cooler and last longer. What matters is:

- **Spacing.** Modules placed too far apart cause dark patches. Placed too close to the face, they create hot spots.
- **Depth of the box.** A slightly deeper box lets the light spread more evenly.
- **Power supply (driver).** A decent driver, kept protected from rain, does more for the life of the board than almost anything else.
- **A timer.** It saves you switching it on and off every evening and keeps the board on during the hours that matter.

## Design that reads from the road

Keep the shop name as the hero. Add one line about what you sell and, if you want, a phone number. That is usually enough. Long lists of services, several phone numbers and small photos rarely read from across the road.

Use strong contrast: dark letters on a light background or light letters on a dark one. Thin fonts and pale colours get lost at night. Ask for a mock-up of the board on a photo of your actual shop front before anything is printed.

## Installation in Dwarka markets

Most installations here are on ground floor fronts, but many shops in the area sit on the first or second floor above the market. Higher boards need ladders or scaffolding, proper fixing into the wall and a safe route for the wiring.

Before we fit, we check:

- What the wall is made of and whether it can take anchors
- Where the power point will come from, and that the connection will be kept dry
- Whether an old board needs to come down first
- Timing, so the work does not block your shop or your neighbours during busy hours

If you are in a residential pocket, or in markets around Najafgarh or Palam where the frontage is shared, it is worth telling your neighbours and the market association before the installation day.

## Quick checklist before you order

1. Get the front measured, or send clear photos with measurements.
2. Confirm the frame material and the face material in writing.
3. Ask how the LEDs will be spaced and where the driver will sit.
4. See a mock-up on your own shop photo.
5. Check any market, landlord or society guidelines on size and lighting.
6. Agree who installs it and who you call if something stops working.
