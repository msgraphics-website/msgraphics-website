An office says something about a company before anyone speaks. A plain white reception and bare glass cabins look unfinished, while a clean logo wall and a few thoughtful graphics make the same space feel settled and confident. Office branding is also one of the few branding jobs your own team sees every day.

Here are the main elements, what each one does and what to check before you start.

## Reception logo wall

The reception is the first thing visitors see and the place most people take photos. A logo wall usually has:

- **Logo letters**: acrylic, metal, or ACP letters, mounted on spacers so they stand off the wall
- **Backlighting or a halo glow**: LED lights behind the letters for a soft outline
- **A backing panel**: wood finish, ACP, fabric or a painted feature wall behind the logo

Keep it simple. The logo and the company name are enough. Check the wall in the evening too, since lighting changes how acrylic and metal look.

## Glass frosting and films

Most offices in Gurgaon, Noida and business centres like Nehru Place have glass cabins and conference rooms. Clear glass causes two problems: people walk into it, and there is no privacy.

Frosting film solves both:

- **Privacy band**: a frosted strip at seated eye level, so people inside are not on display
- **Full frosting**: for meeting rooms and the HR or finance cabin
- **Cut patterns and logos**: the logo repeated along a band, or a pattern that adds some character
- **Printed films**: coloured or printed graphics on glass, for a stronger look

A band at standing eye level also works as a safety marker. Frosting film can be removed later, which makes it a good choice for rented offices.

## Wall graphics

Large blank walls in corridors, the pantry and work areas are good places for graphics. Common options:

- **Values or mission wall**: a few lines about what the company stands for
- **Timeline wall**: the company's journey, useful for offices that host clients
- **Product or work showcase**: photos of real work, which staff and visitors both notice
- **Zone graphics**: naming floor areas or meeting rooms after cities, rivers or themes
- **Wallpaper prints**: printed patterns that cover a whole wall

Vinyl stickers suit smooth painted walls. Printed wallpaper covers larger areas. On textured or freshly painted walls, ask your installer about adhesion first, since paint needs time to cure before vinyl goes on.

Do not overfill. Two or three strong walls are better than graphics on every surface.

## Cabin and directional signage

Signage helps people find their way, especially in larger offices or on multiple floors.

- **Cabin name plates**: name and designation, often on acrylic or metal
- **Meeting room signs**: a name or number for each room
- **Directional signs**: to the reception, washrooms, pantry, fire exits and other floors
- **Floor directories**: near lifts in multi-floor offices

Use the same fonts, colours and materials across all of it, so it reads as one system. For cabin plates, choose a design where the name strip can be changed, since people move cabins.

## Before you start

**Check the lease and the building rules.** Many commercial buildings in Gurgaon and Noida have rules on drilling, external signage and work timings. The landlord or building management may also want walls returned in original condition. Ask them before you plan anything fixed.

**Use correct brand files.** Logo letters and frosting are cut from vector files. A blurry logo pasted from a website will not work. Share the logo as AI, CDR, PDF or SVG.

**Measure properly.** Glass panels vary in size even in the same office. Every pane should be measured on site before films are cut.

**Plan the installation timing.** Most offices prefer installation after hours or on a weekend. Agree the timing with the building's facility team, since vendor entry may need passes.

**Think about the next move.** If you are on a short lease, prefer items that can be removed or reused, like mounted logo letters and films, over painted murals.

## A simple order of work

1. Walk through the office and list every wall, glass panel and cabin
2. Take measurements and photos
3. Prepare designs and mock-ups on the actual photos
4. Approve materials and samples
5. Produce and pre-cut everything
6. Install over one or two sessions
7. Walk through again and fix anything missed
