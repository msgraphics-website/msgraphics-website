A single weekend activation is one thing. A campaign that runs for three or four weeks across many locations is a different job. The kit has to survive daily setup and packing, the team has to reach a new place every morning, and someone has to know at the end of each day what actually happened.

This post is about that second kind of job: a canopy and promo table campaign that moves across markets, housing societies and malls in Delhi NCR.

## Plan the route before the kit

Start with a list of locations and group them by area. A sensible route keeps the team in one zone for a few days at a time: West Delhi and Dwarka, then Gurgaon, then Noida and Greater Noida, then South Delhi. Moving from Rohini to Sohna in a single morning wastes the best hours of the day.

For each location, note:

- Type of place: market, society, mall or office complex
- Best days and hours for footfall
- Who gives permission, and their contact
- Power availability, if you need it for a screen or a light
- Parking or unloading point for the vehicle

Markets are busiest in the evening and on weekends. Societies are good on weekend mornings and evenings. Malls have their own peak hours and their own rules about where you can stand.

## What to brand, and how

The kit gets packed and unpacked every day, so it has to be tough as well as visible.

**Canopy.** The canopy is seen from the furthest distance, so keep it simple: brand name and logo on all sides, large and clear. Avoid putting prices or dates on it, since those change and a canopy is the costliest part to reprint.

**Promo table.** The front panel is at eye level for people walking past, so this is where the offer or the main message goes. A table with a replaceable front graphic lets you change the scheme mid-campaign without new tables.

**Standees.** One or two, placed where people approach from. Use them for the offer, a QR code or a product image.

**Backdrop or side wall.** Useful in malls and wider spaces, but adds setup time. Skip it for tight market spots.

**Promoter uniforms.** T-shirts or jackets in brand colours make the team easy to identify and look more trustworthy.

For a long campaign, ask for a sturdy canopy frame, good carry bags, and a spare set of the table graphic and standee print. Things tear on the second week, not the first.

## Permissions for each type of place

Permissions are the most common reason a team turns up and gets sent back. Rules and charges vary from place to place and change over time, so check locally rather than assuming.

- **Housing societies**: the RWA or facility management office decides the date, spot and any charges.
- **Malls**: the mall marketing or leasing team allots space and sets rules on size, timings and fixtures.
- **Markets**: depending on the spot, this may involve the market association, a shop owner or the local authority. Ask locally.
- **Office complexes**: the facility manager.

Keep a simple sheet with the approval status of every location, and send the team only to confirmed spots. Promoters should carry a copy of the approval on the day.

## Staffing a long campaign

A typical canopy team has two or three promoters and a supervisor who handles more than one team. For a multi-week campaign, think about:

- **Consistency**: keep the same promoters where possible, so they get better at the pitch
- **Briefing**: a short briefing sheet with the one-line message, product basics and common questions
- **Reporting time and location**: shared the evening before
- **Backup staff**: someone always falls sick in a long run
- **Rest days**: tired promoters do not sell well

The supervisor should visit each team at least once a day and send photos.

## Daily reporting

Without a daily report, a campaign is just a set of days nobody can compare. Agree the format before day one. A simple report per location covers:

- Location, date, start and end time
- Photos of the setup and of the crowd at peak time
- Samples, brochures or giveaways distributed
- Leads collected, with the method (form, QR, tablet)
- Any issues: permission problems, weather, late start
- What visitors asked about most

Collect it the same evening, ideally in one shared sheet. By the end of the first week you will see which areas and which types of location work best, and you can shift the remaining days towards them.

## Weather and the Delhi calendar

Plan around the weather. Summer afternoons are hard on both promoters and footfall, and monsoon days need covered spots or a backup date. The festive season brings crowds to markets and malls, but also more competition for space, so book early.
