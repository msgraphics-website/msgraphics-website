The first day at a new job is full of forms, logins and introductions. A well-made welcome kit is one of the few things a new joiner takes home that day, and it sets the tone for how the company sees its people. A thin plastic folder and a cheap pen send one message. A neat box with a few useful things and a proper welcome note sends another.

Here is how to plan a kit that gets used, and how to print it so it holds up.

## Decide what the kit should do

Think about the new joiner's first few weeks. A good kit is a mix of three things:

- **Useful at work**: notebook, pen, bottle, laptop sleeve, desk items
- **Something to wear**: a T-shirt, hoodie or cap that makes them feel part of the team
- **A personal welcome**: a card or letter, ideally signed by the manager or team

For offices in Gurgaon and Noida where many people commute by metro or cab, a good bottle and a laptop bag or sleeve tend to get used every day.

## Items that work well

- **Notebook or diary** with the logo foil stamped or debossed on the cover
- **Pen**, metal or a good-quality plastic, with a laser-engraved or pad-printed logo
- **Bottle or sipper**, laser engraved for metal, screen or UV printed for others
- **Mug**, sublimation printed for coated ceramic mugs
- **T-shirt or hoodie**, screen printed for larger runs, DTF or embroidery for smaller ones
- **Lanyard and ID card holder**
- **Laptop sleeve or tote bag**
- **Desk items**: coasters, a desk calendar, a small plant in a branded pot
- **Welcome card** and a printed one-page guide to the office

Choose five or six items rather than ten. Quality matters more than count, and every extra item is something else to stock and pack.

## Print each item properly

Different materials need different printing methods. Matching them well is what makes the kit look premium.

- **Fabric**: screen printing for simple logos in bulk, DTF for multi-colour or small runs, embroidery for polos, caps and jackets
- **Metal bottles and pens**: laser engraving lasts and looks clean
- **Ceramic mugs**: sublimation gives full colour and lasts through washing
- **Notebooks**: foil stamping, debossing or UV printing on the cover
- **Paper items**: digital or offset printing, depending on quantity

Always ask for a printed sample of each item before the bulk order. Check the logo size, colour and position on the actual product, not just on a mock-up.

## The box and the card

The box is the first thing the new joiner sees. A rigid box with a printed lid, or a printed corrugated box for larger kits, makes a strong first impression. A simple printed sleeve over a plain box also works well.

Inside, use a cut insert or crinkle paper to keep items in place. Place the welcome card on top, so it is the first thing seen when the lid opens.

The card is worth some thought. A short message from the founder or the team, a few useful contacts and the company values on the back make it feel personal. Leave space for a handwritten line from the manager if your team can manage it.

## Plan for ongoing hiring

Welcome kits are not one-time orders. Companies hire through the year, so plan for steady supply:

- **Print in a larger batch** and pack kits as people join
- **Keep a size stock** of T-shirts and hoodies, and refill sizes as they run out
- **Leave personal items for later**: name cards and welcome letters can be printed in small runs when names are known
- **Keep the design flexible**: avoid printing the year or a specific batch name on boxes you plan to use for months

If your company has offices in Delhi, Gurgaon and Noida, or in other cities, decide whether kits are packed centrally and dispatched, or delivered in bulk to each office.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Picking items from a catalogue without seeing a printed sample
- Ordering every T-shirt in one size range
- Logos that are too large and look like advertising
- Paper cards printed on thin stock
- Running out of kits just as a large batch of freshers joins
